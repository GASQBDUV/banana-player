# Play on from the handheld, and the tap a browser wants (2026-11-10)

The handheld can now choose where it plays (START › Playing › **Play on**: the handheld or any open dashboard page),
the other side of the dashboard's switch. The model is in `docs/design/session.md` §12; this is what wasn't obvious.

## How a page gets a name

- Each tab's SSE stream registers `?tab=<TAB_ID>&name=<DEVICE_NAME>&play=0|1` (`events.js`). `WebServer` keeps a
  `TabInfo` per tab (name, can play, first-seen order), so a reconnect keeps its place in the list and a tab gone a
  moment keeps its name (the top card still says "Chrome on Mac" during the 10 s grace). `playTabs()` lists only tabs
  with a live stream, numbering a second of the same name " 2". Names are ASCII-only, 32 chars (the handheld's fonts),
  and are shown nowhere but the handheld.
- `DEVICE_NAME` (`store.js`) reads `navigator.userAgentData` where there is one, else the user agent. iPadOS reports a
  Mac; a Mac UA with `maxTouchPoints > 1` is an iPad **unless** `userAgentData.platform` says macOS: Chromium's touch
  emulation on a real Mac (the headless phone runs) sets touch points too, and read as "Chrome on iPad" before that.
  Checked against sample UAs for Safari iPhone/iPad/Mac, Chrome Mac/Android/iPhone, Firefox and Edge on Windows.

## The refused play()

- A page made the output from the handheld has had no tap, so `el.play()` rejects with `NotAllowedError` (Safari
  always; Chrome on a page with no user activation). Before, the page answered `Pos(playing:0)`: the session went
  paused and the handheld said "Paused in your browser". Now it reports `Blocked`, shows **Tap to play**, and the
  handheld says **Waiting for …** with B as the way out (`takeOver`).
- Two things undid the waiting state in the first run, both fixed in `browser-player.js`:
  - the `seeked` that follows the `loadedmetadata` seek to the session's place calls `sendPos()`, which sent
    `Pos(playing:0)`. `sendPos()` now re-sends `Blocked` (with the place) while blocked and paused.
  - after the tap, `play` fires before `playing` and before `play()`'s promise settles, so `sendPos()` still saw
    `B.blocked` and sent `Blocked` again; the session stayed waiting while the page played. `B.blocked` now clears on
    the `play` event (and `sendPos` only re-sends `Blocked` while the element is paused).
- The page's own Play/Pause words skip while blocked (a browser that fires `pause` after a refusal would otherwise send
  `Pause`). Not seen in Chrome; not verified in Safari.

## Testing it headless

- `Runtime.evaluate` with `userGesture: true` (what the promo helpers' `ev()` does) gives the page user activation,
  which unlocks autoplay: poll without it, or nothing is ever refused.
- `--mute-audio` doesn't make the element muted; Chrome's default headless policy allowed the play anyway. Use
  `--autoplay-policy=document-user-activation-required` to get the refusal; a real tap is `Input.dispatchMouseEvent`
  on `.tapplay .tap-btn`.
- Seen (Mac, headless Chrome 1280 and 390 wide): picker lists "Chrome on Mac"; A moves it at 14.0 s and the page plays
  from 14.0 with the handheld paused; Play on › Handheld brings it back at the page's place; refused → overlay, top card
  "Waiting for Chrome on Mac", the picker's row "Waiting for a tap there"; B → the handheld plays from 13.2; the tap →
  the page plays, `blocked` clears, the top card says "Playing in …". Touch: a tap on the row and on the picker's row.
- A CSS class collision cost a run: `.tp-s` is the transport's small buttons (52 px wide), so the overlay's line
  wrapped a word per line. The overlay's classes are `tap-*`.

## Not verified

- Real Safari on iPhone/iPad (the refusal there, the overlay's tap starting the sound, iPad naming) and the handheld
  itself (the picker's look on the 640×480 panel, the top card's laptop glyph).
